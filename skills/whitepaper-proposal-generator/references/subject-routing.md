## 1. Routing principle

Determine the strategic subject before choosing the proposal sequence. Use the 9 levers as a causal design map, not as nine equal workstreams.

For the selected target lever or bundle:

- study upstream levers until they yield sufficient conceptual requirements;
- design target levers in detail through sublevers, mechanisms, models, rules, roles, metrics, artifacts and rhythms;
- bring cross-cutting and downstream levers into scope only when they enable implementation;
- state deferred levers and the consequences of deferral.

The canonical working chain is:

`strategy -> finance/economics -> client policies/value -> sales/commercial system -> processes -> organization/management -> people/competencies -> culture -> data/automation`.

Treat culture and data/automation as cross-cutting when the evidence requires it; do not force a false linear dependency.

## 2. Lever roles

| Lever | Typical questions | Conceptual requirement examples | Detailed outputs when targeted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategy | Where and how will the company grow? | growth bets, horizons, portfolio priorities, required capabilities | strategic choices, initiatives, goal tree, portfolio and decision rhythm |
| Finance/economics | Is the growth model feasible? | margin, capacity, investment, cost and asset constraints | profit model, budgets, responsibility centers, scenario and metric system |
| Client policies/value | Which clients and promises matter? | segments, jobs, quality, service, product, price and channel requirements | policy set, value propositions, portfolio, service and customer-success model |
| Sales/commercial system | How is demand converted into profitable commitments? | funnel, qualification, offer, role and handoff requirements | sales process, roles, commercial playbook, CRM logic, metrics and review rhythm |
| Processes | How must value be produced repeatedly? | process families, service levels, controls, exceptions and interfaces | L1-L3 map, AS-IS/TO-BE, owners, RASCI, regulations, controls and improvement system |
| Organization/management | Who decides and how do units interact? | accountability, authority, capacity and coordination requirements | target structure, centers, internal contracts/SLA, role passports, meetings and business calendar |
| People/competencies | Can the organization perform the target work? | workforce, competency, hiring, development and load requirements | competency model, assessment, staffing, hiring, onboarding, development and reward systems |
| Culture | Which behaviors make the model work or fail? | leadership, autonomy, collaboration, change and fairness requirements | target behavior model, leadership practices, reinforcement and culture-change route |
| Data/automation | What information and systems must support decisions and work? | entities, master data, decision use cases, integration and control requirements | target data model, IT/CRM requirements, architecture, rollout waves and governance |

## 3. Subject variants

### Integrated/corporate strategy

- Primary target: strategy and economics; optionally policies and portfolio.
- Analysis: any or all nine levers when feasibility and organizational readiness are part of the question.
- Whitepaper thesis: strategy is a set of linked choices and implementation requirements, not a list of aspirations.
- Typical design outputs: growth choices, portfolio, financial scenarios, initiative architecture, target capabilities and governance.

### Commercial strategy

- Primary target: client policies/value and sales; often strategy and economics are co-targets.
- Upstream analysis: growth priorities, profit logic, market/client evidence.
- Cross-cutting design: product/portfolio, price, channel, sales process, CRM, roles and commercial metrics.
- Whitepaper thesis: revenue growth becomes reproducible only when customer choice, economics, policies and sales behavior agree.

### Operational strategy

- Primary target: processes and organization/management.
- Upstream analysis: strategy, economics, client promises and commercial commitments.
- Cross-cutting design: people, culture, data and automation as operating enablers.
- Whitepaper thesis: scale breaks at cross-functional interfaces when processes, accountability and management rhythms lag growth.

### Organizational and management-system transformation

- Primary target: structure, authority, management rhythms, roles and interfaces.
- Upstream analysis: strategy, economics, client promises and process architecture.
- Cross-cutting design: competencies, leadership behavior, dashboards and data.
- Whitepaper thesis: an organization chart works only when units have explicit contracts, decisions, metrics and rhythms.

### People and culture strategy

- Primary target: workforce/competencies and culture.
- Upstream analysis: strategy, economics, client promise, processes and structure.
- Cross-cutting design: leadership model, employee journey, load/capacity, knowledge, reward and decision practices.
- Whitepaper thesis: HR systems are design consequences of the business and operating model, not isolated HR activities.

### Automation, data and IT strategy

- Primary target: data, automation and IT; processes are usually a co-target.
- Upstream analysis: potentially every preceding lever, at conceptual depth.
- Cross-cutting design: decisions, roles, entities, controls, adoption and rollout capacity.
- Whitepaper thesis: technology should mechanize a resolved operating logic and target data model, not automate ambiguity.

### Hybrid transformation

- Select a coherent target bundle and one governing thesis.
- Do not label the route “complex transformation” without naming target levers and the client decision.
- If scope becomes too broad for one decision, recommend a bounded first stage and visible stop/go gate.

## 4. Depth scale

Assign each lever one level:

- `0 out`: consciously excluded.
- `1 context`: read existing materials; no independent conclusion.
- `2 requirements`: focused evidence sufficient to issue conceptual design requirements.
- `3 diagnostic`: multiple sources, contradictions and current-state conclusion.
- `4 design`: sublever-level target model and artifacts.
- `5 implementation`: real cycles, owners, training, correction and acceptance.

Rules:

- upstream levers normally sit at 1-3;
- target levers normally sit at 4;
- implementation enablers normally sit at 4-5;
- a lever at 2 or 3 must end with explicit requirements for the target design;
- a lever at 4 or 5 must end with artifacts, owners, rhythms, metrics and acceptance criteria.

## 5. Stage handoff

### Stage 1 output

Create a design brief containing strategic, economic, client, operating, organizational, human and digital requirements relevant to the target.

### Stage 2 output

Create the target system and implementation package: artifacts, roles, decisions, rules, metrics, rhythms, data and rollout sequence.

### Stage 3 output

Demonstrate independent reproduction in real work. Use the transfer ladder:

`Paper Planes runs -> joint run -> client runs with observation -> client runs independently`.

Do not close the project only because documents were delivered.
