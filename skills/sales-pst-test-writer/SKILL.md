# Sales PST Test Writer

## Core Idea

Create a client-specific PST-style sales test that diagnoses how a salesperson thinks under commercial ambiguity. Do not test whether the candidate knows funnel vocabulary. Test how they notice opportunity, risk, client economics, role boundaries, decision quality, and cognitive traps.

Use the test as a commercial diagnostic product: assessment -> role profile -> risk map -> recommendations -> possible PP service route.

Treat `PST` as an internal product name for a standardized work simulation, not as a standalone psychometric test class. The default high-quality pattern is `role-specific written work sample + structured follow-up interview`; add role play, inbox exercise, or a short defense only when the role requires that behavior.

Do not claim psychometric or predictive validity unless a local validation study exists. Before that, describe the output as a content-valid diagnostic work sample that supports management judgment, development decisions, role-fit hypotheses, and follow-up interviews.

## Required Inputs

Start by collecting or inferring inputs from the client. If missing, use `references/intake-questionnaire.md`.

Minimum viable inputs:

- industry and business model;
- sales cycle and average deal size;
- target roles: Hunter, Farmer, KAM, sales lead, presales, commercial director;
- current commercial problem;
- growth target and margin constraint;
- client types and deal types;
- typical sales process and CRM stages;
- known failure patterns;
- what the client wants to decide after testing.

If fewer than 60% of inputs are available, produce an intake questionnaire first, not a full PST test.

## Build Workflow

1. Define the tested role.
   - Hunter: finds, qualifies, attacks, reframes opportunity.
   - Farmer: protects, develops, stabilizes, expands existing accounts.
   - KAM: manages economic buyer, stakeholders, risk, renewal and expansion.
   - Commercial lead: allocates roles, portfolio, margin, forecast and governance.
   - For non-sales adaptations, define the management role by the decisions it must make, not by the function name. For example, HRD should be tested through workforce strategy, hiring priorities, onboarding, learning, performance, retention, compensation, employer brand, communications, and role-boundary decisions.

2. Build a fictional but industry-plausible company.
   - Make it close enough to the client reality to be diagnostic.
   - Change names and sensitive facts.
   - Include 8-12 facts, not a long case.
   - Mix useful facts, distracting facts, weak signals and contradictions.
   - Remove real personal names from respondent-facing materials unless the user explicitly asks to preserve them. Use role labels such as CEO, administrative director, head of division, HR lead, recruiter, brand manager, or analyst.
   - Provide enough decision data for the requested task. If the candidate must prioritize roles, clients, deals, projects, or interventions, the case must include explicit prioritization criteria and comparable facts: strategic impact, urgency, current gap, dependency on other work, implementation risk, cost or capacity, data quality, and consequence of delay.
   - Do not ask candidates to rank items from vague labels alone. Add a compact case table or fact list that makes tradeoffs possible while still leaving ambiguity.
   - Prefer two short connected cases over one long case when assessing current employees or ambiguous role-fit. Use the first case for diagnosis and decision quality, and the second for prioritization, execution, governance, and data capture. One case is allowed only for a quick screen or when the user explicitly asks for a lightweight test.

3. Insert cognitive traps.
   Include 5-7 traps, never labeling them as traps in the respondent version.

   | Trap | What it tests |
   |---|---|
   | Revenue anchoring | Does the candidate chase gross revenue while ignoring margin, cycle, capacity, payment terms or strategic fit? |
   | Authority bias | Does the candidate obey the CEO / sales director signal without checking evidence? |
   | Loss aversion | Does the candidate protect a bad client because losing them feels painful? |
   | Sunk cost | Does the candidate continue a weak deal because many hours were already spent? |
   | Availability bias | Does the candidate over-weight the loudest recent case? |
   | Confirmation bias | Does the candidate search only for facts supporting the first hypothesis? |
   | Role confusion | Does the candidate mix hunting, farming, presales, project management and customer care? |
   | Forecast optimism | Does the candidate count intent as probability without next evidence? |
   | Client-status illusion | Does the candidate treat an old loyal client as high-potential without checking future economics? |

4. Ask decision questions, not knowledge questions.
   Use 5-6 tasks for a compact respondent-facing PST or 8-12 tasks for a fuller diagnostic:
   - what is the commercial diagnosis;
   - which opportunities to attack first;
   - which deals or clients to stop / downgrade;
   - what questions to ask before action;
   - what CRM / data gaps matter;
   - who should own the next step;
   - how to split Hunter/Farmer/KAM responsibilities;
   - what risks to escalate to management.

5. Score by reasoning pattern.
   Use `references/scoring-rubric.md` if a detailed rubric is needed.
   - Evaluate the sequence `facts -> diagnosis -> trade-off -> decision -> action -> system capture -> review`, not only the final answer.
   - Use non-compensatory critical dimensions when appropriate: a severe failure in evidence discipline, role-boundary clarity, client economics, margin/price awareness, CRM/data thinking, or governance may block a positive conclusion even if the total score looks acceptable.
   - For high-stakes or disputed decisions, recommend two independent raters, rubric calibration, and reconciliation when scores differ materially. The assessed person's direct manager should not be the only rater.

6. Produce two outputs.
   - Respondent package: case, tasks, answer format, timebox.
   - Client package: construct map, answer key, scoring rubric, behavioral anchors, red flags, follow-up probes, role interpretation, commercial recommendations.

7. Add a structured follow-up interview by default for current employee assessment.
   - The follow-up should probe the written answer without rescuing it.
   - Ask the same types of questions across respondents: decisive facts, fragile assumptions, rejected alternatives, data that would change the decision, first 48 hours, owner of next action, accepted risk, and role boundary.
   - Add role play mainly for expert sales and selected brand-sales roles. Add inbox exercise for operational marketing, customer service, coordination-heavy or SLA-heavy roles.

## Output Structure

Use this structure unless the user asks otherwise:

1. `Цель теста`
2. `Кого тестируем`
3. `Что нужно знать о клиенте`
4. `Кейс для кандидата`
5. `Задания`
6. `Что на самом деле проверяется`
7. `Скоринг`
8. `Интерпретация профилей`
9. `Рекомендации клиенту`
10. `Как это продаётся как услуга PP`

## Quality Bar

A strong PST test:

- cannot be solved by choosing socially desirable answers;
- hides the scoring logic from the candidate;
- forces tradeoffs between revenue, margin, risk, capacity and relationship;
- distinguishes attack logic from retention logic;
- reveals role-fit, not just “good / bad salesperson”;
- produces management actions: whom to hire, whom to move, whom to train, what process to change;
- includes enough job fidelity to be a work sample rather than an abstract puzzle;
- has respondent-facing and client-facing packages that differ: candidates see the case and tasks, while the client sees constructs, scoring logic, traps, anchors, red flags, and follow-up probes;
- supports role-fit hypotheses, not deterministic personnel judgments.

## Anti-Patterns

Do not:

- ask “what is a funnel stage?” or other textbook questions;
- make all “good” answers obviously ethical, proactive or client-centric;
- write multiple-choice options where A/B/C map visibly to role types;
- call the traps by name in the respondent version;
- overfit to Сибтехнострой or any one project;
- claim psychometric validity unless a real validation study exists;
- diagnose personality; diagnose observed commercial reasoning in a business simulation;
- let a single total score hide a critical red flag in role boundaries, evidence discipline, economics, data discipline, or governance;
- use one rater's uncalibrated impression as the final assessment in high-stakes cases;
- make a written PST the only basis for role assignment, dismissal, promotion, or hiring when follow-up interview or observed work evidence is available.

## Commercial Packaging

Position the service carefully:

- “Диагностика коммерческого мышления продавцов через бизнес-симуляцию.”
- “Проверяем не знания воронки, а то, как человек принимает решения в сделке.”
- “На выходе: карта ролей Hunter/Farmer/KAM, риски, рекомендации по найму, обучению и перераспределению ответственности.”

Possible PP routes:

- assessment of sales team;
- expert sales implementation;
- sales role redesign;
- CRM and forecast discipline;
- KAM / account development system;
- sales training built on real cases.
