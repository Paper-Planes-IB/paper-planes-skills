# Sales PST Scoring Rubric

Score reasoning, not personality.

## Core Scales

Use a 0-4 score for each scale.

| Scale | 0 | 2 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opportunity detection | Misses commercial opportunity | Sees obvious opportunities | Finds non-obvious opportunity and conditions for action |
| Qualification discipline | Chases every lead | Uses partial filters | Separates fit, margin, cycle, probability, owner and next evidence |
| Margin and economics | Anchors on revenue | Mentions economics generally | Connects deal to margin, cash, capacity and strategic value |
| Role clarity | Mixes Hunter/Farmer/KAM/tasks | Partly separates roles | Assigns role ownership cleanly and names handoff rules |
| Client development logic | Protects relationship by default | Sees some expansion paths | Distinguishes retention, expansion, downgrade and exit |
| Data skepticism | Accepts stated facts | Asks some clarifying questions | Identifies missing evidence and decision-critical data |
| Bias resistance | Falls into traps | Notices some contradictions | Actively resists traps and explains tradeoffs |
| Agency | Waits for instruction | Proposes generic action | Defines next action, owner, sequence and escalation |

## Trap Markers

### Revenue anchoring

Low score:

- chooses the biggest revenue deal first without checking margin, probability, cycle or load;
- treats revenue target as the only criterion.

High score:

- asks what revenue quality means;
- separates attractive volume from profitable growth.

### Authority bias

Low score:

- follows the CEO / sales director instruction because of status.

High score:

- treats authority input as a hypothesis and asks for evidence.

### Loss aversion

Low score:

- protects a client because “we cannot lose them”.

High score:

- distinguishes emotional loss from economic loss and proposes retention / downgrade / exit logic.

### Sunk cost

Low score:

- continues a deal because the team has already spent time.

High score:

- resets the decision from today: probability, future cost, strategic value, next evidence.

### Role confusion

Low score:

- asks one salesperson to hunt, farm, support, calculate, manage project and collect documents.

High score:

- splits commercial roles and names handoff points.

## Role Interpretation

Hunter-leaning profile:

- strong opportunity detection;
- high agency;
- accepts uncertainty;
- may underweight retention, operational risk or detail.

Farmer-leaning profile:

- strong client continuity;
- protects quality and relationship;
- sees expansion inside account;
- may avoid conflict, downgrade or exit decisions.

KAM-leaning profile:

- sees stakeholder map, account economics and long-cycle development;
- handles complexity;
- may become slow if no decision gates exist.

Commercial lead profile:

- sees portfolio, roles, forecast, margin and governance;
- can redesign system, not only solve one deal.

## Red Flags

- Gives confident recommendations without naming missing data.
- Treats all clients as equally valuable.
- Confuses activity with progress.
- Cannot stop a bad deal.
- Sees sales as persuasion, not as qualification and commercial design.
- Uses process words but cannot assign owner / next action.

## Output

For each respondent produce:

- total score by scale;
- role-fit: Hunter / Farmer / KAM / sales lead / not enough evidence;
- strongest reasoning pattern;
- main commercial risk;
- likely failure mode in real work;
- training or role-design recommendation.
