# Product Growth Hypothesis Superpro

## Overview

This skill turns vague product analysis into a disciplined growth loop:

1. Diagnose the bottleneck precisely
2. Pick the economic or behavioral lever
3. Generate 2-3 hypothesis variants, not one generic idea
4. Define the test shape and success threshold
5. Recommend the next move after the result: stop, refine, scale, or amplify

The core principle: a good hypothesis is not just "what to build", but "why this lever, for which cohort, through which mechanism, measured by which change".

## When to Use

- Funnel metrics feel static or repetitive
- The same 5-7 hypothesis patterns keep recurring
- A product needs stronger activation, engagement, retention, pricing, or monetization ideas
- A tested hypothesis needs a serious "what next?" recommendation
- A PM/Growth screen should act like a senior product analyst instead of a rules-only dashboard

## Diagnostic Order

Always work in this order:

1. `Constraint`
   - What metric is actually underperforming?
   - Is the problem traffic, activation, depth, conversion, or retention?
2. `Cohort`
   - Which users are affected?
   - New, warm, paid, at-risk, inactive, post-pricing visitors, incomplete viewers?
3. `Mechanism`
   - What behavior must change?
   - Start, return, complete, compare, trust, choose, pay?
4. `Surface`
   - Where can the change happen?
   - Onboarding, catalog, pricing, account, notification, Telegram, content CTA?
5. `Economic link`
   - How does this move a business metric?
   - MRR, ARR, annual share, free-to-paid, completion, goals fill, revisit rate?

## Output Standard

Every strong hypothesis should contain:

- `Observation`: current fact pattern with enough numbers to justify urgency
- `Hypothesis`: explicit causal claim
- `Action`: concrete product or trigger change
- `Target metric`: one primary metric
- `Secondary metric`: one validating or guarding metric
- `Confidence`: why we believe this might work
- `Expected impact`: how big the move could be

## Variant Rule

Do not stop at one idea per metric.

For an important metric, generate variants across different mechanisms:

- `UI / choice architecture`
- `Notification / follow-up`
- `Personalization`
- `Trust / proof`
- `Friction removal`
- `Sequencing / onboarding`

Example:

- low pricing conversion:
  - personalize pricing
  - follow up after pricing visit
  - add proof / FAQ / objections

- low completion:
  - reminder after partial watch
  - continue-watching rail
  - next-best recommendation after return

## Scale-Up Rule

`Scale` does not mean "archive as success".

If a hypothesis worked, the next question is:

- how do we amplify it?
- how do we remove the next bottleneck?
- what adjacent mechanism compounds the gain?

After a successful test, always generate one `scale-up hypothesis`:

- same bottleneck, stronger mechanism
- or next bottleneck unlocked by the first win

Examples:

- personalization on pricing worked -> add proof and objections handling
- annual pricing framing worked -> make annual the default choice
- catalog goals banner worked -> add follow-up sequence outside catalog
- reminder to finish video worked -> add resume rail and next-best recommendation

## Anti-Patterns

Reject hypotheses that are:

- generic and reusable for any product
- not tied to a visible bottleneck
- not measurable
- only "send more notifications" without cohort, trigger, and mechanism
- only "improve onboarding" without specifying which step, which friction, and what the new behavior should be

## Quality Check

Before accepting a hypothesis, ask:

1. What exact behavior changes?
2. Why should this cohort react differently?
3. Where in the product or communication does the change happen?
4. Which metric should move first?
5. What is the next hypothesis if this one wins?

If two or more of these are vague, the hypothesis is not ready.
