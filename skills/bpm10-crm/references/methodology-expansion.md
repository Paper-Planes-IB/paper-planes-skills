# BPM10 Methodology Expansion

Use these blocks when the task extends beyond a first CRM sketch. They generalize reusable lessons from completed B2B CRM work without carrying client-specific data.

## 1. Strategic Alignment

Before designing CRM, answer:

1. Which strategic goal must CRM help manage?
2. Which channels, segments, products, territories, and client states differ materially?
3. Which KPIs must CRM feed or explain?
4. Which management forum uses the data?
5. Which action must follow a deviation?

Do not project CRM as a sales interface alone. Connect it to strategic priorities, commercial model, role structure, and management cadence.

## 2. Problem Map And Problem Graph

Use the problem map as the first artifact when interviews reveal fragmented issues. Do not copy interview wording into solution requirements.

Normalize each issue into:

- fact;
- risk or constraint;
- causal relation;
- management criterion;
- design implication;
- evidence and confidence.

Use a graph when problems cross at least three layers: strategy, sales/marketing, roles/processes, data/systems, analytics/governance.

## 3. Client Types And Lifecycles

Separate client types only when they differ by at least one material design variable:

- lifecycle;
- owner;
- result;
- mandatory evidence;
- control points;
- analytics;
- system carrier.

If differences affect reporting but not work, use a type/subtype field rather than a separate smart process.

Do not collapse different management objects such as a holding, legal entity, branch, site, and installed asset when relationships and actions depend on the distinction.

## 4. 4R Refinements

Split Reach into inbound and outbound when stages, owners, or evidence differ.

Treat MQL as basic relevance and routing. Treat SQL as readiness for an active commercial opportunity. Confirm the exact qualification criteria with the client.

Do not force Refresh into the first implementation. A relevant but inactive company may be managed by a pause/reason/return rule inside another process if this is simpler and sufficient.

Re-Engage must manage a change in account state, not a list of periodic calls. Link each development hypothesis to a commercial object, evidence, owner, and next decision.

## 5. Marketing And Service Processes

Model non-sales processes only when they have a distinct controlled result. Examples:

- event participation or training;
- POSM or campaign support;
- product launch at an account;
- service incident, complaint, audit, repair, or warranty case.

Do not represent every activity as a funnel. Use tasks or activities when no independent lifecycle, owner, result, or analytics is required.

## 6. System Carrier Decision

For each major process, answer:

| Question | CRM implication |
|---|---|
| Does it have an owner, status, stages, and next action? | Likely a CRM/service card process |
| Does it manage customer communication? | CRM needs a card or linked activity |
| Is it a mass transaction? | Keep facts in ERP/warehouse; send exceptions to CRM |
| Is it legally/accounting significant? | Keep record in ERP/document flow; pass status/link to CRM |
| Does management need exception control? | BI exposes the deviation; CRM receives or tracks the action |
| Is it experimental and low-volume? | A temporary table may be acceptable with owner and end date |

## 7. Governance

Require a CRM owner with authority over:

- launch and adoption;
- field and stage changes;
- data quality rules;
- user feedback;
- integration incidents;
- management reporting;
- training and discipline.

Create RASCI by object, not only by department. Cover funnel, data, decision, checkpoint, dashboard, and change request.

## 8. Management Rhythm

A CRM review must use approved system facts. An oral status without a card or confirmed source is not a management fact when the action is supposed to live in CRM.

Each review item ends with:

- decision;
- owner;
- due date;
- system where the action lives;
- closure evidence.

## 9. Dashboard As Control Loop

Each dashboard signal must answer:

1. What deviated?
2. Where: client, channel, region, funnel, product, or object?
3. Who owns the explanation and action?
4. What is the likely or confirmed reason?
5. What action exists or must be created?
6. Where does the action live?

If the dashboard cannot start or verify action, it is reporting, not a complete management contour.

## 10. Versioning And Pilot

Separate:

- current state;
- approved target pilot;
- later target state;
- rejected ideas;
- unresolved questions.

Pilot the smallest coherent contour that includes an object, owner, lifecycle, data, action, review, and closure. Do not pilot disconnected fields or decorative dashboards.
