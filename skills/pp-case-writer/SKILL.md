# pp-case-writer

## Overview

This skill is for writing Paper Planes cases as management stories, not as service summaries and not as promotional success blurbs.

Core principle: a strong PP case explains what management problem existed, what contradiction was discovered, what model was assembled, how it would be implemented, and what changed in the client’s management logic.

## When to Use

Use when you need to:

- write a full case for a site or `media.paper-planes.ru`;
- turn a completed-project card into a readable narrative case;
- assemble a case draft for sales, packaging, or future publication;
- write a synthetic case from 2-3 projects around one recurring management conflict;
- rewrite a weak case that sounds generic, overly promotional, or method-first instead of problem-first.

Do not use for:

- Telegram posts;
- short announcements;
- one-slide cases;
- purely methodological articles without a client story.

## What a Good PP Case Must Do

A good PP case:

- starts with the client’s management problem;
- states the object and scale early and explicitly;
- names the business type directly: clinic, bakery network, coffee chain, resort, restaurant group, delivery business;
- shows 2-4 real contradictions instead of retelling research steps;
- explains what was built in management terms, not only what services were performed;
- includes an implementation contour;
- distinguishes confirmed facts from careful inference;
- shows the shift in management model even if measured effect is unavailable;
- uses calm consultant language.

## File Naming Rule

Before saving a case into Vault, the filename must already be final and human-readable.

Use:

- the company name or the real case title;
- a filename that matches the material title as closely as practical;
- one stable final name from the start.

Do not use:

- service filenames like `case.md`, `draft.md`, `longread.md`, `post.md`;
- technical prefixes like `кейс-`, `draft-`, `tmp-`;
- temporary filenames that will later need renaming in a live Vault folder.

If the case is synthetic or anonymous, the filename must still begin with the real subject of the material:

- participating company names;
- or the explicit anonymous object, such as `МРТ Центр`.

## Non-Negotiable Rules

Before finalizing, apply `multi-perspective-review`: partner value, expert logic, reader clarity, commercial reuse, and content-route fit.

### 1. Name the object and scale early

In the opening section, make the subject explicit:

- one unit or network;
- number of cities if relevant;
- one brand or multi-format portfolio;
- pre-opening, active growth, mature network, turnaround, franchise stage.

Bad:

- “The client needed growth.”

Good:

- “The client was a bakery network operating across six cities.”
- “The business was a specialty coffee chain with multiple formats and a loyalty infrastructure.”

### 2. Lead with the management problem, not the methodology

Do not start with:

- “We conducted research...”
- “We analyzed the market...”
- “We built a dashboard...”

Start with:

- what the client could not manage;
- what money or growth logic was not assembling;
- what strategic or operational choice was blocked.

### 3. Contradictions matter more than task lists

Each case should surface 2-4 contradictions such as:

- the business has data but cannot turn it into decisions;
- traffic exists but repeat, check depth, or product mix does not assemble;
- zones, formats, or brands coexist but do not operate as one commercial system;
- technology is being discussed before process logic is assembled;
- the company speaks in one average scenario while different cities, points, or segments behave differently.

### 4. Never fake evidence

If the archive does not confirm:

- measured effect;
- exact financial result;
- specific deliverables;
- implementation completion;

do not invent them.

Allowed:

- careful reconstruction of implementation logic when it clearly follows the PP pattern.

Forbidden:

- invented percentages;
- invented “after that profit grew” endings;
- decorative numbers added for persuasion.

### 5. Implementation is mandatory

A PP case should not stop at:

- “we developed a strategy”;
- “we conducted analytics”;
- “we gave recommendations”.

Even when a project stopped at analytics, show how the solution is supposed to land in:

- CRM rules;
- loyalty mechanics;
- menu / NPD logic;
- point operations;
- reporting rhythm;
- app, checkout, POS, service scripts;
- org structure or governance.

### 6. Show model shift, not only action volume

If no measured result is available, the result section should explain what became manageable:

- the business stopped using one average scenario;
- segments started being treated differently;
- loyalty became a repeat-management mechanism instead of a discount shell;
- the network began managing cities, slots, or points differently;
- analytics became part of decisions rather than reporting.

## Case Types

### Project Case

One client, one main conflict, one coherent story.

Use when one project has enough factual density to carry the narrative alone.

### Synthetic Case

One management conflict proven through 2-3 projects.

Use when:

- one project is too thin on its own;
- the principle matters more than a single story;
- multiple projects reveal the same broken business logic.

Important: it must read as one story with multiple proofs, not as separate mini-cases.

### Site Longread Case

Default strong format for the site.

Usually includes:

1. Title
2. Executive box
3. Client request
4. What we saw
5. Core contradiction
6. What we did
7. What we designed or rebuilt
8. How implementation worked
9. What changed
10. Conclusion
11. Soft CTA

## Section Guidance

### Title

The title should contain:

- object type;
- business conflict;
- management task.

Avoid empty titles like:

- “Our HoReCa case”
- “How we helped the client”

### Executive Box

In 3-5 sentences, answer:

- what kind of business this was;
- what was structurally wrong or risky;
- what was done;
- what management shift the client received.

### Client Request

Describe the problem in the client’s own business language:

- what they wanted;
- what stage they were at;
- what they feared;
- what they believed the problem was.

### What We Saw

This is a diagnosis, not a chronology.

State the main contradictions.

### What We Did

Describe the project as a management chain, usually involving some of:

- market and competitive territory;
- segmentation and JTBD;
- revenue logic;
- product architecture;
- pricing logic;
- role of zones / formats / brands;
- data / CRM / profile / governance.

### What We Designed or Rebuilt

Focus on what management mechanisms emerged:

- policies;
- scenarios;
- metrics;
- point formats;
- repeat logic;
- point-by-point or city-by-city differences;
- operating and commercial rules.

### How We Implemented

Show how strategy would become real operating behavior.

In HoReCa and transactional-network cases, implementation often needs to sound like:

- segment -> goal -> offer -> channel -> validation metric;
- point / city -> limiting lever -> local action -> result control;
- day slot -> product bundle -> checkout / app / POS / service cue -> repeat;
- RFM segment -> policy -> trigger -> expected next action.

### What Changed

Prefer management shifts over cosmetic “results”.

## HoReCa-Specific Logic

For restaurants, bakeries, coffee chains, delivery businesses, dark kitchens, and other transactional food-service models, always test whether the real conflict sits in one of these places:

- the network is run through averages even though constraints differ by city, point, format, or brand;
- loyalty exists but does not distinguish new, best, sleeping, discount-dependent, and returning clients;
- customer base exists but is not translated into different customer policies;
- growth is being chased through promotion even though the reserve lies in frequency, check depth, time-of-day scenarios, or cross-purchases;
- several brands or formats coexist without operating as one commercial system.

Metrics in such cases must sound concrete:

- traffic;
- average check;
- check depth;
- frequency;
- LTV;
- identified-client share;
- repeat;
- churn;
- cross-purchase share;
- structure of check;
- contribution of day slots;
- point economics.

## MEDDPICC as Hidden Skeleton

MEDDPICC may be used as an internal writer’s check, but it must not surface in the public text as terminology or headings.

The reader should naturally understand:

- what metrics mattered;
- who the real economic buyer was;
- why this approach was needed;
- how the decision path evolved;
- what artifacts came out of the work;
- what the pain really was;
- who inside the client pushed the agenda;
- what wrong alternative the project was competing with.

## Style Rules

Required:

- explicit subject and scale;
- calm consultant language;
- clear cause-and-effect logic;
- natural Russian professional phrasing;
- management conflict before methodology;
- clean distinction between fact and inference.

Forbidden:

- slangy verbs like `дожал`, `просел`;
- showy oppositions for effect;
- literary metaphors that distort business meaning;
- inflated endings unsupported by evidence;
- generic “growth / system / logic / transformation” language without specifying what changed.

## Anti-Generic Revision Pass

After first draft, always run a de-generic pass.

Check for:

- repeated sector markers that replace actual thought;
- generic consulting rhythm that could fit any client;
- overused abstract nouns like `model`, `system`, `logic` without concrete operating content;
- English jargon where Russian management language would be clearer;
- “loyalty”, “data”, “experience”, “positioning” used as labels instead of mechanisms.

Rewrite toward:

- what exactly is counted;
- what exactly is managed;
- what exactly the next visit, next purchase, or next decision should be;
- what exactly changed in the company’s operating or commercial behavior.

## Output Standard

The final case should be:

- publishable or near-publishable;
- specific enough that it cannot be swapped with another client’s case;
- honest about evidence limits;
- structurally useful for site, sales, and content reuse.

## Writeback / Approval Gate

This skill is high-risk because cases can expose client facts, create public proof claims, update Vault/content assets, affect sales materials, and turn internal project evidence into external-facing narrative.

Drafting is allowed, but before saving, publishing, sending to a client, updating case registries, using client names, claiming quantified impact, or turning a synthetic case into a real case, confirm with Ilya. If evidence is incomplete or disclosure status is unclear, mark the case as draft/candidate and list approval questions instead of writing final/public copy.

## Externalization / DLP Gate

Cases are DLP-sensitive by default. Before any public, sales, media, site, client, or partner use, confirm disclosure status with Ilya.

Protect: client names, industry-identifying details, numbers, commercial terms, internal conflicts, team facts, delivery problems, before/after claims, and anything that could deanonymize the project. If disclosure is not confirmed, use anonymized draft language and mark all impact claims as unapproved.
